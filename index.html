Classic arcade games, each reduced to its rules on a small grid

Cabinet of Distilled Pixels

Each card reduces a classic arcade game to the rule that makes it play: who moves, what threatens, what is worth reaching. Every mechanic runs on the same small grid with the same five colours in two shades each, so you can compare Pong with Breakout, or Pac-Man with Bomberman, at a glance.

The demos are scripted with simple heuristics. Each episode ends on a win or a loss, holds for a moment, then restarts with a new seed.

Each colour comes in two shades. Full is the thing itself; dim is its trail, body, shot or weaker state. A red square where the player was marks a loss.

How to read a card

The rule sheet names what each colour stands for in that game. The year is the first release of the game that defined the mechanic; earlier precursors are listed alongside it.

Why a grid

A tiny grid strips away art, sound and scale, so what is left is the logic: movement rules, collisions, spawns and scoring. Mechanics that look nothing alike on screen often turn out to be close relatives here.

What comes next

Each simulation exposes its state and a tick function, so any card can later take keyboard input in place of its scripted agent.
